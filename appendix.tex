\section{Robustness: Cohort Heterogeneity, Observable Selection, and Answer Quality}
\label{sec:robustness_revision}

Table~\ref{tab:cohort_robustness} addresses the concern that pooling entry cohorts from 2008 through 2025 conflates structurally different eras of the platform (reputation inflation, tag evolution, and the post-2022 generative-AI disruption). Because calendar year enters the matching design only as an exact stratum, treated and control questions within a pair always share a year; to probe heterogeneity \emph{across} years we re-estimate Model~A on the pre-2020 subsample, both pooled and in the newcomer ($<$\,1 week) stratum. The pooled pre-2020 arrival hazard ratio is 1.03 (versus 1.03 in the full sample), and the newcomer association is essentially unchanged (arrival HR\,$=$\,1.07 pre-2020 versus 1.07 in the full sample), indicating that the tenure gradient was already present before the generative-AI era.

\input{analysis/output_tables/cohort_robustness.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:observable_controls} reports the pooled Model~A after adding pre-treatment observable selection controls (posting hour and weekday, number of tags, body and title length, and asker reputation). View count is \emph{not} included: it accrues after posting and is higher for answered questions, so conditioning on it would open a post-treatment / collider channel already excluded from the propensity score. The pooled arrival increment remains essentially unchanged once these pre-treatment observables are held fixed (arrival HR\,$=$\,1.03, versus 1.03 in the baseline specification).

\input{analysis/output_tables/observable_controls.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:newcomer_robustness} reports the newcomer ($<$\,1 week) bucket under several specifications. The baseline arrival increment is 1.07. Adding the same pre-treatment observable controls --- still \emph{without} view count --- further strengthens the newcomer association (arrival 1.13), so the association is not an artifact of observable selection. Adding answer-quality controls, by contrast, pushes the newcomer increment below one (arrival 0.95), the same pattern seen in the pooled effect and reinforcing that answer quality --- not observable selection --- is the most consequential confound the design does not resolve.

\input{analysis/output_tables/newcomer_robustness.tex}

Because each row already conditions on a response-time window, the quality-controlled fits use Model~A covariates plus those three signals rather than continuous log-response-time interactions. Comparing columns shows how much of the bin-wise arrival pattern survives once observable quality is held fixed.

To assess the robustness and consistency of my findings, I conducted a series of robustness checks that varied the observation length, the construction of the dependent variable \textit{numHelped}, or model specification.

\section{Implementation Details} All models are estimated using the \texttt{lifelines} CoxTimeVaryingFitter with a small ridge (L2) penalizer applied for numerical stability. The penalizer strength is $\lambda = 5 \times 10^{-3}$ for main-effect models and $\lambda = 10^{-2}$ for models that include response-time interaction terms (where the interaction covariates are correlated with the base treatment indicator). These values are sufficiently small that they introduce negligible shrinkage relative to the scale of the estimated effects, but they ensure stable Hessian inversion in large risk sets. All covariates are mean-centered before fitting; response-time interaction terms are additionally clipped to their 5th--95th percentile range and z-scored as described above. Cox-table standard errors are model-based (inverse-Hessian) and are \emph{not} cluster-robust; dependence from matched pairs, reused controls, and repeated intervals can make those intervals too narrow. For the pooled Model~A DiD we therefore also report a matched-pair bootstrap 95\% CI (100 replicates resampling whole \texttt{match\_id} pairs) in Table~\ref{tab:pair_bootstrap}. Ridge penalization and the winsorizing and standardization of the interaction covariates additionally regularize the reported model-based standard errors. For the pooled experienced-user models (H1), point estimation uses a random subsample of approximately 8~million user-interval rows drawn from matched pairs (preserving pair integrity) when the full interval frame exceeds the fit cap; table \textit{Events} counts still refer to the full analysis sample. Tenure-bucket-specific models (H2, H3) use all available intervals within each stratum.
