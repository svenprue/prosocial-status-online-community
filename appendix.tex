\section{Robustness: Cohort Heterogeneity, Observable Selection, and Answer Quality}
\label{sec:robustness_revision}

Table~\ref{tab:cohort_robustness} addresses the concern that pooling entry cohorts from 2008 through 2025 conflates structurally different eras of the platform (reputation inflation, tag evolution, and the post-2022 generative-AI disruption). Because calendar year enters the matching design only as an exact stratum, treated and control questions within a pair always share a year; to probe heterogeneity \emph{across} years we re-estimate Model~A on the pre-2020 subsample, both pooled and in the newcomer ($<$\,1 week) stratum. The pooled pre-2020 arrival hazard ratio is 1.04 (versus 1.05 in the full sample), and the newcomer association is essentially unchanged (arrival HR\,$=$\,1.07 pre-2020 versus 1.07 in the full sample), indicating that the tenure gradient was already present before the generative-AI era. Because calendar year enters as an exact-matching stratum, the pre-2020 subsample is not disjoint from the full sample but a large sub-population of it, retaining 79\% of matched rows and 91\% of helping events (Table~\ref{tab:cohort_robustness}). This check is therefore compositional --- it confirms the association does not depend on the post-2020 cohorts --- rather than an independent replication on separate data.

\input{analysis/output_tables/cohort_robustness.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:observable_controls} reports the pooled Model~A after adding pre-treatment observable selection controls (posting hour and weekday, number of tags, body and title length, and asker reputation). View count is \emph{not} included: it accrues after posting and is higher for answered questions, so conditioning on it would open a post-treatment / collider channel already excluded from the propensity score. The pooled arrival increment remains essentially unchanged once these pre-treatment observables are held fixed (arrival HR\,$=$\,1.04, versus 1.05 in the baseline specification).

\input{analysis/output_tables/observable_controls.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:answer_quality_robustness} reports the pooled speed-specification arrival increment as the three answer-quality controls (first-answer length, vote score, and acceptance) are entered one at a time. The gradient is informative about what each signal absorbs: answer length --- the least endogenous signal, fixed by the answerer at composition --- barely moves the estimate (1.038 baseline to 1.032); vote score brings it to 1.010; and the full specification, adding acceptance, pushes it below one (0.982), with the same pattern in the newcomer stratum (0.95; Table~\ref{tab:newcomer_robustness}). Because vote score and, especially, acceptance are post-treatment and endogenous to the outcome (accepting requires the asker to return to the site, the same re-engagement that produces helping), these level estimates are a bounding exercise rather than a causal decomposition: the association is robust to the terse-versus-detailed concern (length), and it is reversed only by the signals a bad-control logic predicts would over-adjust.

\input{analysis/output_tables/answer_quality_robustness.tex}

Table~\ref{tab:newcomer_robustness} reports the newcomer ($<$\,1 week) bucket under several specifications. The baseline arrival increment is 1.07. Adding the same pre-treatment observable controls --- still \emph{without} view count --- raises the arrival increment to 1.13, which indicates the newcomer association is not an artifact of \emph{observable} selection. This gain warrants caution rather than reassurance, however: the waiting-period diagnostic rises in step, from $\beta_2 = +0.050$ to $+0.122$, essentially the magnitude of the arrival log-increment itself ($\log 1.13 = 0.121$). Under these controls, treated newcomers already diverge upward from their matched controls by nearly the full effect size \emph{before} the answer arrives, so the two terms inflate together; we therefore read the specification as ruling out observable selection as the sole driver, not as independent corroboration of the arrival increment, and it sharpens the parallel-trends caveat that already attaches to this stratum. Adding answer-quality controls, by contrast, pushes the newcomer increment below one (arrival 0.95), the same pattern seen in the pooled effect and reinforcing that answer quality --- not observable selection --- is the most consequential confound the design does not resolve.

Table~\ref{tab:score_coding_sensitivity} probes whether the answer-quality result depends on how the vote-score control is coded. The specifications in Table~\ref{tab:answer_quality_robustness} enter the score linearly (winsorized at the 5th and 95th percentiles and standardized); here we re-estimate the score-only and full-quality specifications, pooled and in the newcomer stratum, under two alternatives --- a signed-log ($\mathrm{asinh}$) coding and ordinal bins (1--2, 3--9, and $\ge 10$ votes, reference $\le 0$) --- at full data. We use $\mathrm{asinh}(\mathrm{score})$ rather than $\log(1+\mathrm{score})$ because roughly two million answers carry net-negative (downvoted) scores, for which $\log(1+\mathrm{score})$ is undefined. The below-one full-specification result is robust to the coding: the pooled arrival increment is 0.98 under all three codings and the newcomer counterpart 0.95, and if anything the better-specified signed-log and binned codings pull the estimate marginally \emph{further} below one rather than back toward it. The one estimate that does not survive re-coding is the intermediate score-only step, which is significantly above one under the linear coding (1.01) but statistically indistinguishable from one under the signed-log and binned codings; we therefore do not read it as evidence of an above-one effect.

\section{Implementation Details} All models are estimated using the \texttt{lifelines} CoxTimeVaryingFitter with a small ridge (L2) penalizer applied for numerical stability. The penalizer strength is $\lambda = 5 \times 10^{-3}$ for main-effect models and $\lambda = 10^{-2}$ for models that include response-time interaction terms (where the interaction covariates are correlated with the base treatment indicator). These values are sufficiently small that they introduce negligible shrinkage relative to the scale of the estimated effects, but they ensure stable Hessian inversion in large risk sets. All covariates are mean-centered before fitting; response-time interaction terms are additionally clipped to their 5th--95th percentile range and z-scored as described above. Before each fit, interval boundaries are rounded to a one-hour grid (interval start and stop times to the nearest hour) and zero-length intervals produced by the rounding are dropped, so multiple helping events by the same user within the same hour of a window collapse to a single counted event. This is the ``time rounding'' referenced in the table notes; all reported \textit{Events} counts are computed after it. It is also why two families of event counts differ by construction: the raw mean of 0.18 help events per window (Table~\ref{tab:desc_stats}) corresponds to 0.1411 counted events per row in the estimation sample ($3{,}435{,}973/24{,}349{,}760$), and the composite-outcome Events (Table~\ref{tab:outcome_decomposition}) are smaller than the sum of their component channels because same-hour events across channels likewise merge. Cox-table standard errors are model-based (inverse-Hessian) and are \emph{not} cluster-robust; dependence from matched pairs, reused controls, and repeated intervals can make those intervals too narrow. For the Model~A DiD we therefore also report matched-pair bootstrap 95\% CIs (resampling whole \texttt{match\_id} pairs) in Table~\ref{tab:pair_bootstrap} --- 100 replicates for the pooled effect and 500 for the newcomer ($<$ 1 week) stratum that carries the headline result. For the pooled models (H1, spanning all experience levels), the reported point estimates are fit on the full interval frame (the fit cap is raised for the point-estimate fits), so no subsampling is applied; the default cap of approximately 8~million matched-pair user-interval rows (preserving pair integrity) applies only to the pooled bootstrap replicates, whereas the smaller newcomer stratum is fit in full within each replicate. A single 8~million-row subsample proved seed-fragile for the pooled arrival increment, which is why the reported estimate comes from a full-data fit. Table \textit{Events} counts refer to the full analysis sample throughout, and the estimation-sample sizes are tabulated in Table~\ref{tab:estimation_samples}. Tenure-bucket-specific models (H2, H3) use all available intervals within each stratum.
