\title{Help Converts Newcomers, Not Veterans: Generalized Reciprocity and Platform Engagement on Stack Overflow}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent Generalized reciprocity --- the tendency to help others after receiving help oneself --- is widely theorized as a mechanism sustaining cooperation on online knowledge-sharing platforms, yet robust field evidence remains scarce. Survey self-reports struggle to separate reciprocity from other prosocial motives, and observational designs confound it with baseline user activity, producing upward-biased estimates. We develop a matched difference-in-differences survival analysis of a large-scale sample of Stack Overflow questions, comparing users whose questions received an answer with propensity-score-matched users whose questions did not, and tracking each user's helping of others before and after the answer. Users who receive an answer help others at a modestly higher rate afterward, and this elevation is largest among newcomers and declines to near zero among the most experienced users --- a pattern consistent with reciprocity operating as a contributor-recruitment mechanism. The design also surfaces an important qualification: much of the divergence between treated and control users emerges while they await an answer, before any help is received, so part of the apparent effect reflects the heightened activity of users whose questions attract answers rather than a response to help itself. We report the treatment effect together with this pre-answer divergence, find only weak and inconsistent moderation by answer speed, and delimit what these patterns do and do not establish about reciprocity in field settings. The study contributes a more conservative estimate of generalized reciprocity and a design that makes its central confound visible.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Ethics statement}
This study analyzed de-identified, publicly available archival records from Stack Overflow. No interaction with human participants occurred, and no personally identifiable information was collected or analyzed. Ethical approval was not required under applicable institutional policies for secondary analysis of public platform data.

\section*{Data availability}
Stack Overflow public data dumps were obtained from the Internet Archive (\url{https://archive.org/details/stackexchange}). Analysis code and step-by-step instructions to reproduce all tables and figures are available in a public repository (URL withheld for blind review). Processed analytical datasets used in the matching and survival analyses are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.
