\noindent We submit the original research article ``Help Converts Newcomers, Not Veterans: Generalized Reciprocity and Platform Engagement on Stack Overflow'' for consideration as a research report in \textit{Computers in Human Behavior}.

\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent The paper examines whether receiving help on an online platform increases subsequent helping behavior. Using a matched difference-in-differences survival analysis of more than 21 million questions from Stack Overflow, we find that receiving an answer increases the hazard of later helping by roughly 6\% overall, but the effect is concentrated among newcomers and disappears among the most experienced users. Response time moderates the effect non-linearly: reciprocity peaks when answers arrive 30--60 minutes after a question is posted, consistent with a platform re-engagement mechanism. We believe this work fits naturally within the journal's \textit{Cyberbehavior} area on human behavior in virtual spaces, as it examines how computer-mediated help-seeking and help-giving shape cooperation on a large online platform.

\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent We believe the manuscript will interest readers working on online communities, prosocial behavior, knowledge sharing, and the human consequences of platform design. The study contributes new field evidence on generalized reciprocity and offers actionable implications for contributor recruitment on question-and-answer platforms.
