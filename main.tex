%% ----- Abstract ------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
\noindent Whether receiving help on an online platform increases subsequent helping is an open empirical question. Prior observational studies report mixed results, and designs that do not net out baseline user activity may overstate reciprocity. We develop a matched difference-in-differences survival analysis of over 21 million Stack Overflow questions, comparing users whose questions received an answer with propensity-score-matched users whose questions did not. Receiving an answer is associated with a small increase in the hazard of subsequently answering other users' questions (difference-in-differences increment at answer arrival, hazard ratio [HR]\,$=$\,1.05, 95\% CI [1.04, 1.05]), and this association is steeply graded by user tenure: it is strongest among newcomers (HR\,$=$\,1.07 in the first week) and fades to zero among the longest-tenured users. The newcomer gradient is the most robust pattern in the data: the first-week association also holds on the pre-2020, pre-generative-AI subsample. Faster answers are associated with a larger arrival increment, with a pronounced early peak for answers arriving within 30--60 minutes --- qualified support for an immediacy account, though we cannot fully separate answer speed from answer quality. The data identify short-run behavioral associations, not the psychological mechanisms (gratitude, moral obligation, incentive displacement) that theory proposes. The pattern is consistent with generalized reciprocity operating primarily as a contributor-recruitment channel at the point of entry, before platform-specific incentives take hold, and it suggests newcomer-focused design implications.
\end{abstract}
