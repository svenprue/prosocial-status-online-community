%% ----- Abstract ------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
\noindent Whether receiving help on an online platform increases subsequent helping is an open empirical question. Prior observational studies report mixed results, and designs that do not net out baseline user activity may overstate reciprocity. We develop a matched difference-in-differences survival analysis of over 21 million Stack Overflow questions, comparing users whose questions received an answer with propensity-score-matched users whose questions did not. The association is small and steeply graded by user tenure. At the pooled level it is modest and statistically marginal (arrival-increment hazard ratio [HR]\,$\approx$\,1.00; summed difference-in-differences upper bound 1.01), but the increment at answer arrival is clearest among newcomers (HR\,$=$\,1.06 in the first week; summed upper bound 1.11) and fades to zero --- or slightly below --- among users with more than three years of experience. This newcomer gradient is the most robust pattern in the data: the first-week association also holds on the pre-2020, pre-generative-AI subsample. Faster answers are associated with a larger arrival increment, with a pronounced early peak at 30--60 minutes that persists after conditioning on answer quality --- qualified support for an immediacy account, though we cannot fully separate answer speed from answer quality. The data identify short-run behavioral associations, not the psychological mechanisms (gratitude, moral obligation, incentive displacement) that theory proposes. These findings provide a better-identified short-run behavioral test of generalized reciprocity in one large technical Q\&A community and suggest tentative, newcomer-focused design implications.
\end{abstract}
